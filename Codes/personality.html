Personality

Personality Traits

My three strongest personality traits are:

  • Analytical
  • Resilient
  • Compassionate

Analytical

I naturally like to understand how things work rather than simply accepting information at face value. I ask questions, examine situations and try to find logical solutions. This is particularly important in my academic life, especially as a Computer Networking and Cybersecurity student, where critical thinking and problem-solving are essential.

Resilient

Challenges are part of growth, and while things may not always go according to plan, I try to find ways to keep moving forward. Balancing full-time employment with university studies has taught me the importance of determination, patience and adaptability. My resilience helps me adjust to unexpected challenges without losing sight of my academic goals.

Compassionate

I believe that everyone faces challenges that may not always be visible to others. While I value responsibility and accountability, I also believe in showing understanding and consideration. In my academic life, this helps me approach group assignments with empathy, listen to different perspectives and support classmates who may be experiencing difficulties, while still working towards our shared goals.

My Favourite Quote

"I will love the light for it shows me the way, yet I will ensure the darkness for it shows me the stars."

Author: Og Mandino

About the Author

Og Mandino was an American author best known for his motivational writing, including The Greatest Salesman in the World. His work often explored perseverance, personal growth and overcoming life's challenges.

Why Is This Quote Meaningful to Me?

This quote reminds me that there is value in both the good and difficult seasons of life. It is easy to appreciate the moments when everything seems to be going according to plan, but I believe that some of the greatest lessons come from the moments that challenge us the most.

To me, the darkness represents those periods when the way forward is not always clear, while the stars represent the lessons, opportunities and strength we may discover along the way. I appreciate this quote because it encourages me to remain hopeful, even when circumstances are not ideal, and to recognise that difficult experiences can still contribute to my personal growth.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Education

Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools such as ChatGPT, Claude and Microsoft Copilot have changed how students access information and approach learning. While these tools can make academic tasks easier, I believe their value depends on how they are used. There is a difference between using AI to help us understand something and depending on it to think or complete our work for us.

Productive Struggle vs. Unproductive Friction

One idea that stands out to me is the distinction between productive struggle and unproductive friction. South (2026) explains that productive struggle involves the effort required to understand concepts and develop knowledge, while unproductive friction involves obstacles that consume time without necessarily contributing to learning. I find this particularly relevant when approaching an unfamiliar topic or assignment. Sometimes, before I can begin researching or completing a task, I first need to understand what is being asked of me. In those situations, I use AI to break down instructions, explain unfamiliar terminology in simpler language and help me identify the expected deliverables. For me, that is not avoiding the work. It is finding a starting point that allows me to engage with it more effectively.

How Can AI Enhance Learning?

AI can be useful as a learning aid when it provides explanations, hints and guidance rather than simply generating completed answers. Wang et al. (2025) explore this distinction in STEM education, examining whether AI functions as a scaffold that supports learning or a crutch that replaces students' efforts. In my own academic experience, I prefer asking for a step-by-step explanation when I struggle with a programming concept. Understanding why something works, identifying where I went wrong and being able to repeat the process independently are more valuable to me than receiving a solution I cannot explain. I also use AI to organise lecture materials into study guides, generate revision questions and conduct practice quizzes. These activities encourage me to revisit information, identify gaps in my understanding and assess what I have actually learned.

When AI Becomes a Crutch

However, convenience can become a disadvantage when students rely on AI to complete tasks without engaging with the underlying concepts. Research discussed by Holmes (2026) and Mineo (2025) raises concerns about overreliance and whether improvements in AI-assisted performance translate into independent learning. I believe students must remain responsible for questioning AI-generated responses, verifying information and developing their own conclusions. AI can provide a convincing explanation and still be incorrect. It should therefore complement lecture materials, independent research and practical experience rather than replace them.

My Personal View

I do not believe the goal of education should be to make every task effortless. Some challenges are necessary because they develop our ability to think critically, solve problems and apply knowledge. At the same time, I see no benefit in remaining confused when a tool is available to help make a difficult concept more understandable.

For me, AI is most effective when it helps me move from not knowing where to begin to being able to understand, attempt and eventually complete a task independently. The distinction is simple: I want AI to help me learn how to arrive at an answer, not become dependent on it to provide every answer.

Ultimately, AI should strengthen our ability to learn, not weaken our willingness to think.
